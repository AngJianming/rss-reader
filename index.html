They could trigger false transactions, corrupt data, or worse. HMAC (Hash-Based Message Authentication Code) lets you cryptographically sign each webhook with a shared secret: You and the webhook consumer share a secret key (known only to both) You compute an HMAC-SHA256 hash of the webhook body using that secret You send the hash as a header (usually X-Signature or X-Webhook-Signature) They recompute the hash on their end—if it matches, the webhook is authentic Signature = HMAC-SHA256(webhook_body, shared_secret) Send as: X-Signature: sha256= The consumer verifies by computing the same hash and comparing it to the header value. A mismatch means tampering or forgery.
