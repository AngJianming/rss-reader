Here’s my step-by-step approach: HTTP sends everything in plain text (yes, even your passwords). HTTPS encrypts data during transit, preventing man-in-the-middle attacks. 👉 Rule I follow: Never send sensitive data over HTTP. OAuth 2.0 handles authorization. OpenID Connect (OIDC) adds authentication. I enforce the principle of least privilege: Users only get access to the exact endpoints they need. 👉 This keeps the attack surface small and manageable. Early on, I made the mistake of setting Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *. Now, I explicitly whitelist trusted domains (e.g., https://myapp.com). 👉 No wildcard * in production. Rate limiting protects against DDoS, scraping, or accidental overload. I apply limits per API key or per IP/user. 👉 Example: Free users = 5 requests/sec, Premium users = 15 requests/sec.
