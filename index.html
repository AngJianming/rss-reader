This guide is about building that intuition systematically. In traditional art, you have infinite colors. In pixel art, you have constraints. Most pixel artists limit themselves to 4-16 colors per sprite. This constraint forces intentionality. A good palette makes a sprite look professional. A bad palette makes even excellent linework look amateur. The most reliable palette structure: build from light to dark within ONE hue. For a red element: Highlight: light red Midtone: pure red Shadow: dark red This gives you 3 steps. Add a fourth for extreme light or shadow, and you have a complete base palette. Start every sprite with just 3 colors: A light tone A midtone A dark tone Draw the whole sprite in 3 colors. If it looks good, you are done. If something feels missing, add a 4th color. Pixalo includes palette management tools to lock in your colors before drawing. Pair colors from opposite sides of the color wheel: Red + green Blue + orange Yellow + purple Use sparingly — one complementary pair per sprite is usually enough. Low saturation for shadows, full saturation for midtones and highlights. This mimics how light works in the real world and gives your art depth. Once you have 4-8 colors: Draw a simple shape (sphere, cube) Check if it reads at 32x32 pixels If it works small, your palette is solid Lospec has an incredible library of curated palettes from professional pixel artists.
